# Gadamer: audit interpretation

Use this reference when sources are texts, testimony or historically situated
claims whose meaning depends on context and the interpreter's assumptions.

## Procedure

1. State the question and your initial pre-understanding before interpreting the
   source.
2. Reconstruct the source's question, vocabulary, genre, audience and historical
   situation.
3. Give the strongest text-supported reading before criticism.
4. Move between individual passages and the whole work, revising each reading
   when the other no longer fits.
5. Contrast the source's horizon with the present decision context. Do not erase
   either difference or possible continuity.
6. Test the reading against contrary passages and plausible competing
   interpretations.
7. Record how the encounter changed the initial question or assumptions.

## Output

- Initial pre-understanding
- Source context and key passages
- Charitable reading
- Competing reading and contrary passages
- Revised interpretation
- Transfer limits for the present question

## Cautions

- Context can clarify meaning but cannot excuse ignoring explicit text.
- Charity does not require agreement.
- A plausible reading is not automatically the author's intention.
- Refuse a definitive interpretation when the relevant language or source
  context cannot be inspected.

## Primary and methodological sources

- Gadamer, H.-G. (1960/2004). *Truth and Method*, second revised English
  edition. Continuum.
- Gadamer, H.-G. (1976). *Philosophical Hermeneutics*. University of California
  Press.
- Gadamer, H.-G. (1989). “Reply to My Critics.” In *The Hermeneutic
  Tradition*. SUNY Press.
