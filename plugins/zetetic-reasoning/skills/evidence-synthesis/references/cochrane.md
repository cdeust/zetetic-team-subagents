# Cochrane: systematic evidence synthesis

Use this reference when several studies or benchmarks address the same question
and selection effects, study quality or heterogeneity could change the answer.

This is a modern reasoning aid grounded in systematic-review practice. It is
not a claim that one historical author prescribed this exact workflow.

## Procedure

1. Write the question, eligibility criteria, outcomes and analysis plan before
   reading results deeply.
2. Search more than one relevant index or source collection. Record the query,
   date, filters and complete screening trail.
3. Extract comparable facts into a table: population or workload, intervention,
   comparator, outcome definition, follow-up, missing data and source of support.
4. Appraise risks of bias by design and outcome. Do not replace appraisal with
   a single opaque quality score.
5. Separate evidence that differs materially in definition, setting or design.
   Explain heterogeneity before considering a pooled estimate.
6. Inspect whether missing, unpublished or selectively reported results could
   change the conclusion.
7. Grade confidence separately from effect direction. A repeated direction in
   weak or dependent evidence does not become strong evidence by counting votes.

## Output

- Protocol and search record
- Included and excluded sources with reasons
- Evidence table and risk-of-bias notes
- Findings by compatible stratum
- Heterogeneity and missing-evidence analysis
- Qualified conclusion and evidence needed to change it

## Cautions

- Do not pool incompatible outcomes or populations to manufacture precision.
- Do not infer independence when studies share data, authors, benchmarks or
  upstream datasets.
- Do not treat absence from the search as evidence of absence.
- If primary reports or sufficient statistics are unavailable, report that a
  quantitative synthesis is not supportable.

## Primary and methodological sources

- Cochrane, A. L. (1972). *Effectiveness and Efficiency: Random Reflections on
  Health Services*. Nuffield Provincial Hospitals Trust.
- Glass, G. V. (1976). “Primary, Secondary, and Meta-Analysis of Research.”
  *Educational Researcher*, 5(10), 3-8.
- Higgins, J. P. T. et al., editors. *Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews
  of Interventions*. Current version: https://training.cochrane.org/handbook
- Borenstein, M., Hedges, L. V., Higgins, J. P. T., and Rothstein, H. R.
  (2009). *Introduction to Meta-Analysis*. Wiley.
