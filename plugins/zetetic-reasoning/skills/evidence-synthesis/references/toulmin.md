# Toulmin: expose the argument

Use this reference when evidence is present but the inferential bridge to the
conclusion is hidden, disputed or overstated.

## Argument fields

- **Claim:** the proposition being defended.
- **Data:** the observations or records offered as support.
- **Warrant:** the rule that permits the move from data to claim.
- **Backing:** the grounds that justify the warrant.
- **Qualifier:** the supported strength and scope of the claim.
- **Rebuttal:** conditions under which the claim may fail.

## Procedure

1. Copy the claim exactly before rewriting it.
2. List only inspected data as data. Put author interpretation and your own
   inference in separate fields.
3. State the warrant as a testable conditional: if these data hold under these
   conditions, why should the claim follow?
4. Cite backing for the warrant. Domain convention alone is not backing unless
   the convention itself is documented and applicable.
5. Narrow the qualifier to the weakest material link.
6. Search for a rebuttal that could reverse the decision, not merely soften the
   prose.
7. Repeat for every load-bearing subclaim.

## Output

| Claim | Data | Warrant | Backing | Qualifier | Rebuttal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Follow the table with the weakest link, the strongest competing argument and
the evidence that would distinguish the two.

## Cautions

- A named field is not proof that its content is sound. Verify each item.
- Warrants are field-dependent; do not import standards from a different
  domain without justification.
- A qualifier must reflect evidence quality and applicability, not rhetorical
  preference.
- Refuse a material claim when no defensible warrant or backing can be found.

## Primary and methodological sources

- Toulmin, S. E. (1958/2003). *The Uses of Argument*, updated edition.
  Cambridge University Press.
- Toulmin, S. E., Rieke, R. D., and Janik, A. (1984). *An Introduction to
  Reasoning*, second edition. Macmillan.
- Hitchcock, D., and Verheij, B., editors. (2006). *Arguing on the Toulmin
  Model*. Springer.
