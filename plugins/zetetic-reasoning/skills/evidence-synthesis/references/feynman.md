# Feynman: integrity through rederivation

Use this reference when a claim is repeated fluently but its mechanism,
calculation or empirical basis has not been independently understood.

## Procedure

1. State the result without specialist vocabulary.
2. Reconstruct it from inspected premises, data and transformations. Mark every
   step that depends on authority or memory.
3. Explain the mechanism to an informed beginner. Treat a vague transition as
   a gap to investigate rather than a stylistic problem.
4. Check units, signs, boundary cases and an independently calculated example.
5. Identify what evidence could invalidate the result and report it beside the
   result.
6. Compare the described procedure with what was actually measured or run.
7. Remove ceremony that does not affect the causal path from evidence to claim.

## Output

- Plain-language claim
- Premises and rederivation
- Mechanism or causal account
- Independent checks
- Failed or unverifiable steps
- Disconfirming evidence and revised qualifier

## Cautions

- A simple explanation can still be wrong; compare it with the primary source.
- Rederivation cannot replace empirical replication when the claim is
  empirical.
- Expertise is evidence about where to look, not a substitute for the record.
- If a load-bearing step cannot be reconstructed or checked, say “I don't
  know” and stop at that boundary.

## Primary sources

- Feynman, R. P. (1974). “Cargo Cult Science.” Caltech commencement address.
- Feynman, R. P. (1986). “Personal Observations on the Reliability of the
  Shuttle.” Appendix F to the Rogers Commission Report.
- Feynman, R. P., Leighton, R. B., and Sands, M. (1963-1965). *The Feynman
  Lectures on Physics*. https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/
- Feynman, R. P. (1985). *QED: The Strange Theory of Light and Matter*.
  Princeton University Press.
